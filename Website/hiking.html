Hiking

Explore Scenic Trails and Reconnect with Nature

From mountain ridges to ocean coastlines, these hiking spots offer something for every explorer.

Washington Trails

  • Rattlesnake Ledge (Near North Bend)
    Distance: 4 miles round trip
    Elevation Gain: 1,160 feet
    Highlights: A moderate hike leading to a rocky outcrop with panoramic views of Rattlesnake Lake and surrounding mountains.
  • Snow Lake Trail (Alpine Lakes Wilderness)
    Distance: 7.2 miles round trip
    Elevation Gain: 1,800 feet
    Highlights: Offers alpine lake views, summer wildflowers, and winter snowshoeing.
  • Hurricane Ridge (Olympic National Park)
    Distance: Varies (Hurricane Hill Trail is 3.2 miles round trip)
    Elevation Gain: 650 feet
    Highlights: Accessible alpine meadows with sweeping views of the Olympic Mountains and the Strait of Juan de Fuca.

Oregon Trails

  • Timberline Trail (Mount Hood)
    Distance: 40.7 miles loop
    Elevation Gain: 9,000 feet
    Highlights: Encircles Mount Hood with alpine meadows, glacial rivers, and dense forests.
  • Oregon Coast Trail
    Distance: Approximately 425 miles
    Highlights: Follows the full Oregon coastline with beach walks, forested headlands, and coastal towns.

British Columbia Trails

  • Garibaldi Lake (Garibaldi Provincial Park)
    Distance: 11.6 miles round trip
    Elevation Gain: 2,600 feet
    Highlights: Turquoise glacial lake surrounded by snowy peaks — great for photography and camping.
  • Berg Lake Trail (Mount Robson Provincial Park)
    Distance: 26 miles round trip
    Elevation Gain: 2,600 feet
    Highlights: Through diverse ecosystems to Berg Lake beneath Mount Robson (tallest peak in the Canadian Rockies).

Long-Distance Trails

  • Pacific Northwest Trail (PNT)
    Distance: 1,200 miles
    Route: From Glacier National Park (Montana) to the Pacific Ocean (Washington)
    Highlights: Crosses many mountain ranges and national parks — a complete regional experience.
  • Pacific Crest Trail (PCT) – Northern Sections
    Distance: ~1,000 miles through Washington and Oregon
    Highlights: Traverses the Cascade and Sierra Nevada ranges — from desert to alpine forests.
